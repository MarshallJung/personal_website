All-In

One of my favorite podcasts to listen to is the All-In Podcast where @chamath, @jason, @davidsacks, @friedberg cover all things economic, tech, political, social, and poker. Aside from the poker part of the discussions (which are thankfully few and far between) it’s very high alpha. Unique in the way they have constructed a “team of rivals” they often disagree but it does get me thinking outside my own bubble and that’s always a good thing.

In the latest episode here: https://music.youtube.com/watch?v=w30WLkNU47g&si=np_Qj_Tj_ut9YzWJ&t=1248 they talk about the “bubble” in AI investment. In particular they referenced this blog by David Cahn at Sequoia Capital (https://www.sequoiacap.com/article/ais-600b-question/) where the the summary might be that the margin level and pace of AI revenue is simply not going to meet investors dreams. “In today’s environment the revenue expectations implied by the AI infrastructure build-out, and actual revenue growth in the AI ecosystem, which is also a proxy for end-user value. I described this as a $600B[sic] hole that needs to be filled for each year of CapEx at today’s levels.””

One could argue that this is the last dying breath of ZIRP cash and VC money desperate to find some sort of return, but I think it’s hard to argue that there has been a real game changing, population wide AI tool that has made an impact. Those of us at the edge of the e/acc bubble might be really leveraging these tools on a daily basis, but we are still in the very early phases of the AI tool revolution and despite how cool this is, it’s going to take a while and there will be some real wipeouts in the meantime. But of course this means opportunity!

One thing that I’d correct the All-In folks with is the misunderstanding of dependance on Nvidia for GPUs. Yes, they are the 800 pound gorilla in the GPU chip space, but Google trained Gemini on its own systolic array ASIC TPUs which are way, way cheaper. I have a feeling that NVIDIA stock will have a reckoning at some point. (Yes, yes woe to me who called the top back six months ago. Don’t follow my investing advice)

A tweak to my concepts

https://bmcbioinformatics.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/1471-2105-12-334

So one of the difficulties that I’ve found in understanding how to store and generate 3D objects using transformer based models is how to store the training data. Sure, 3D vector space is how we all usually think about objects in the real world, but size of this data and the indeterminate scale and orientation is something that needs to be done in a data pipeline ahead of time. This challenge combined with the observation that one “unit” of an object has effects, continuity or relationships with those around it lead me to this spatial indexing paper that explores 3D interactions in the micro-world. Think about being able to encode the 3D relationships in a transformer layer in a way that adheres to physical laws, but keeps the benefits of language model generation.

I’ve already said it

arxiv.org/abs/2406.05184

Will training on AI-generated synthetic data lead to the next frontier of vision models? The key idea being, synthetic data isn’t truly new data. All synthetic data derives from the original (real) data used to train a generative model. To quantify the actual “value-add” of synthetic data, it must be compared against using this original data directly. In this paper they make this comparison explicit. On five image recognition benchmarks, they compare CLIP models fine tuned on either: Synthetic images generated Stable Diffusion, which was trained on the LAION-2B dataset or original real images directly retrieved from LAION-2B. Overall, they show that while training on synthetic data can improve models (which is consistent with earlier findings from other research), training on original real data helps much more across all settings/scales considered. With current best methods, synthetic images do not yield added value.
