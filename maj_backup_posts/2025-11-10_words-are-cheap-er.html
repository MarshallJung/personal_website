Words are Cheap(er)

I’ve often written in my newsletter that as AI becomes an easier everyday assistant, authenticity will grow more valuable, not less. This might seem counter-intuitive in a world where AI can effortlessly generate high-quality text, but the core of our interactions, whether in the professional sphere or in a personal note, remains fundamentally human. Even if AI can replace vast portions of the work itself, we still deal with other people. Whether in the office, on a video call, or through thoughtful emails to friends and colleagues. The value shift lies not in the production of content, but in the intent and personal voice behind it.

This academic paper (https://jesse-silbert.github.io/website/silbert_jmp.pdf) argues that the written word has become far cheaper, both in terms of time and cognitive effort, thanks to large language models. This shift has significant implications for signaling theory. Signaling, in this context, refers to the observable actions, outputs, or characteristics that convey information about an individual’s hidden qualities. This could be produced work, such as a well-written report or a piece of art, or even non-output signals like coherent speech, which traditionally signaled that a person was intelligent, hardworking, and diligent.

When a meticulously crafted email takes 10 minutes of genuine human effort, it is a strong, costly signal of diligence and respect for the recipient. If that same quality email can be generated by an LLM in 10 seconds, the signal is devalued. The cost of the signal (the writing) no longer reliably reflects the hidden quality (the diligence or effort). This phenomenon pushes the market for authenticity to a new equilibrium. As AI handles the mechanical aspects of production, what becomes the new, costly signal?

I don’t see a way around this. As I mentioned a few weeks ago, most blog content is already AI-generated. If we keep treating the volume and polish of written output as a proxy for thoughtfulness, effort, and time, we’ll have to shift our signaling back to personal interaction. At least for now, it’s the one thing AI can’t replace.

To realize AI’s promised productivity gains, we should remove meaningless, burdensome work and spend more time face-to-face with other humans. That, I think, is the broad direction for AI-driven productivity benefits.

The Low Hanging Fruit in Marketing

https://labs.google.com/pomelli/about/

Based on your website URL, this new Google Labs experiment can help you build effective campaigns tailored to your brand and business identity. Obviously your website should be up to date and solid in branding already, and if so this offers a quick way to generate scalable, on-brand content for social media and other advertising. Just another interesting AI application that is transforming the creative space. Will it replace the need for a marketing/ads agency to do the work? Probably not entirely, but it will allow faster iteration.

The other side of this coin is that it lowers the cost of content which will increase the supply substantially, and if there is one thing that we don’t need it’s more adds. However I think the ultimate iteration of this will be to build and deliver customized content for particular segments in targeted advertising campaigns. That might be a great ROI for a consumer based org to adopt.

Upskilling Your AI

As some my readers are already aware, Claude Skills are customizable folders containing instructions, scripts, and resources that enhance Anthropics AI for specific tasks. They package expertise to turn Claude into a task-specific specialist. You use them by placing documents in a single folder; Claude automatically identifies and loads the relevant skills based on the task — no manual selection required.

Users create skills with Claude’s internal skill tool, which guides workflow setup and generates the folder structure. Custom skills can also be uploaded directly.

This GitHub repo provides an open-source Python tool that converts documentation, GitHub repos, and PDFs into ready-to-upload Claude skills via the skills tool. A standout feature is its AST-based code analysis and conflict detection, which flags discrepancies between outdated docs and current implementations which helps address documentation drift. The tool supports large-scale processing with async scraping and auto-categorization, enabling sub-skills for massive docs. It’s particularly effective for improving prompt efficiency in large, production-grade AI knowledge bases. Take a look here: https://github.com/yusufkaraaslan/Skill_Seekers/

Set Aside Some Time

https://huggingface.co/spaces/HuggingFaceTB/smol-training-playbook

The authors tell you to set aside 2–4 days to read and work through this blog/book. So this isn’t a small undertaking! Hugging Face’s “Smol Training Playbook” distills four years of practical LLM training experience into a 200+ page guide, covering pre-training ablation strategies, post-training optimization, and infrastructure resilience based on SmolLM3’s month-long run on 384 H100s.

The playbook addresses real-world challenges like noisy loss curves and NCCL debugging — often omitted from academic papers — offering actionable rules for hyperparameter scaling and data curation to achieve state-of-the-art small models.

Released amid growing open-source AI efforts, the guide’s rapid 1.5M views and community threads underscore demand for transparent, reproducible pipelines that bridge research gaps in efficient LLM development.

If you’ll need a fine‑tuned language model for an enterprise application, this playbook is a great place to start to understand exactly what you’ll need to make it happen.

Playing Politics

As my work with larger enterprises grows, I’m seeing a phenomenon Ethan Mollick (https://x.com/emollick) noted: AI’s role in blurring traditional job boundaries, allowing entrepreneurial workers to span design, coding, and marketing, sparking under-discussed conflicts in organizations. Responses range from defensive retrenchment against cross-role intrusions to proactive restructuring that accelerates projects by rethinking team dynamics. A recent study in Innovative Human Capital (https://www.innovativehumancapital.com/article/ai-driven-role-conflict-navigating-capability-expansion-and-territorial-tensions-in-the-generative) describes this as an emergent tension driving capability expansion, with outcomes hinging on leadership’s ability to navigate territorial disputes over expertise.

As more people who inherently know how to maximize their productivity with AI gain influence, organizations will need to rethink how they reward employees with remuneration and promotions. Skill sets that drive advancement won’t always match traditional criteria. This shift in what makes a great employee can breed animosity, but leadership must identify these contributors, give them opportunities to succeed, and educate others to acquire the same skills so everyone feels they’re competing on an equal playing field.
