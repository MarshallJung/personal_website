FUD in AI

Fear, Uncertainty and Doubt. Eliciting these emotions sells news. Humans tend to react more vigorously to negative emotions than positive ones, and we all know that we ourselves have fallen for the angry clickbait headlines. This phenomenon is well understood (​​https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1908369116) and has only gotten worse over the last few years.

Aside from the individual health effects of negative emotions (https://www.medicinenet.com/how_do_negative_emotions_affect_physical_health/article.htm), anger and despair harm our relationships and cooperation to build a caring and successful society. So what are we to think of the recent Goldman article claiming that hundreds of millions of jobs will be severely affected if not replaced by AI? Is this simply more fear baiting or is there some truth to the article? In this short piece I hope to explore the concerns behind the article. There are some technical issues to be optimistic about an AI future and more importantly there are human reasons to be hopeful.

Building trust and confidence in AI

Goldman recently published an article claiming that AI will lead to the elimination of hundreds of millions of jobs. However, there are reasons to remain optimistic about the future. This article will delve into the concerns raised in this article and discuss both technical and human reasons for hope in an AI-driven future.

Let’s address the largest and most unrealistic claim that our current AI tools will somehow become sentient. I can understand how this is commonly misperceived. The complexity of the responses from these tools is impressive. We should expect nothing less from an enormous model running on shockingly expensive infrastructure and trained with a data set so large that it cannot be reviewed by humans. Never in the history of technology has so much money been devoted to a single software experiment. But one only needs to search Twitter for the hashtag #GPTFails to see the other side of this tool. GPT fails as often as it succeeds but those examples simply don’t get the same publicity.

The other mitigating issue here is that in order for businesses to replace the at risk jobs noted in Goldman’s report a given business would have two choices. Either pay to use a public API interface of some variety or clone/create their own LLM tool. The first option is dangerous because the data given to a public API is now part of that model’s training data. If I can retrieve and summarize Shakespeare and all the centuries of literary analysis via GPT4, why should I think that the valuable and secret enterprise code business engineers are uploading and modifying is going to be private? But building a GPT equivalent tool locally to mitigate data leakage requires not only deep ML knowledge but to host a sufficiently sized model a company would have to spend millions of $$ per year. That’s an ROI that is awfully hard to make work for most enterprises. Technology will advance over time, but for the foreseeable future the workable tools will be much smaller and less capable. They will be suited to help in narrow and repetitive tasks that are well defined, explainable and trusted. This will only serve to enhance worker productivity, not replace humans.

The second, and equally important aspect to the future of AI is that of human psychology. With time and advancements in computing technology Generative AI might well be “technically” capable of replacing human beings but it won’t for the considerably more simplistic reason of human psychology. There will always be a need for humans to work together to build something wonderful. This means managing and growing people. Emotions, nuance, humor and repair are all items needed to build something greater than each of us could on our own. Putting an AI as a replacement teammate is not something that will build confidence or camaraderie. An AI is not part of a team brainstorming session. There is no inherent creativity, and ultimately the lack of trust in the answers an AI gives is tough to overcome. When GPT gets something really wrong on a crucial decision, how likely is a team to trust the answers in the future? Ultimately this affects company culture. Too heavy a reliance, whether in practice or concept is a recipe for a difficult company success story. Let’s be cautious and celebrate companies that thoughtfully enhance employee productivity rather than seek to replace it.

So as we wrap up this piece, let’s complete the circle. Yes, AI will make a difference in our lives, that is certain and cannot be reversed. But the doomed outlook of an enervated society completely reliant on technology is something that is not in our future. Human beings are still the most wonderful, capable, creative and beautiful resource we have to build businesses or communities. AI will help for sure, but it will not replace us. Take heart and love one another.
