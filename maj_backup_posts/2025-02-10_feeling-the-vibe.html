Feeling the Vibe

A bit of a shift in the world of ML/AI this week. Yes, there were new models from Google (and a ripoff of Google with OAI’s “Deep Research”), but I’m talking more about the gut feeling, reactions and opinions from the AI-o’sphere. The “vibe” as the kids say. It started with this tweet:

Now we all know that Andrej is not one prone to dry wit or tongue-in-cheek poasting, so the intention is valid. And it isn’t just the vaulted thought leaders of the AI space that are hinting at the fusion of thought and LLMs that gets me wondering. The first thought process that crosses my mind when I have a task to accomplish is “How do I use AI to do this?”. I have been gifted with a whole new box of super-tools that can be chained together to make the task faster, more repeatable or both. Maybe it’s building a strategy around prototyping a PoC or marketing/branding a new backend framework for universal deployment. Deep Research query + Grok for cutting edge thought -> Into NotebookLM to synthesize the disparate sources -> Combine that synthesis with existing code base and documentation in a long context reasoning model -> Prompt with a detailed outline (which I dictated via voice and had AI consolidate and clarify) -> move products into Cursor/Figma/Github/Jira. What used to take me many hours would be done in the space of 45 minutes. It’s like a super power.

Simultaneously there is an entire part of X/Twitter (that is usually incredibly adjacent to crypto for some reason) that got absolutely one shotted by LLMs. It’s like a constant cacophony of technobabble and schizophrenia.

Not to mention the endless “NeXT GreAt THinG!!”/”MODEL X WAS RELEASED JUST 8 HOURS AGO AND YOU WON’T BELIEVE THE THINGS PEOPLE ARE DOING WITH IT. HERE ARE THE TOP 10 THINGS THAT WILL BLOW YOUR MIND!!” accounts that seem to have reach, might just be paid influencers, but never really deliver much value.

So am I a bit tired of all this? Yea, a bit. These tools are awesome, but they are not omnipotent, and will not replace all the workers in some random knowledge based field. Maybe that’s in the future, but right now they are just tools. Good tools, but you still have to know how to use them.

How to Use Them

So the hype is real. AI has mesmerized a certain group of folks that don’t understand the algorithms or limitations and simultaneously enabled a group of grifters to capitalize on the fear of AI. But @jeremyphoward on X has some good advice:

“If you’re a knowledge worker you should be really deeply engaging with this technology in a very practical, grounded way. Talk to lots of other people in your space, find the very best people who are working with AI in the very best way and then try and do it even better. Have groups of friends who are interested in pushing this, see how far you can take it. Combine it with your areas of interest and expertise. This is what we focus on now at Answer.AI. Recognize it’s really hard to use AI. It looks easy because there’s a text box and you can type English into it, and it’s like talking to a person. But it’s pretending to be a person, it’s actually a computer program, it’s a very big and complex computer program. So using it correctly takes months and months of diligent study and practice. When you start doing it, you will be shit and you will get bad results. That’s because you’re shit at it, not because AI doesn’t work.

Yeah, be a tinkerer. When we hire people at Answer.AI we specifically say we’re looking for tinkerers. Now is a great time to be a generalist.”

I found a pretty good summary of what AI is good at today: https://x.com/burkov/status/1886895572495269951
I think it’s a good place to start. But remember that these are tools, and not all tools are especially obvious in how you might use them most effectively. There is a difference between a hammer and a lathe…

Some light entertainment

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7xTGNNLPyMI

Here is a lovely 3 hour video that is a great summary for a general audience deep dive into the Large Language Model (LLM) AI technology that powers ChatGPT and related products. It covers the full training stack of how the models are developed, along with mental models of how to think about their “psychology”, and how to best use them in practical applications. I found it to be well done and really drives home the basics. It might allow you to sort the “vibe” from the grift!

They are not the answer to Everything

https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2502.00032

The Weaviate team (not a small company by any means) released this paper which I can generously describe as “hitting databases with a lead (LLM) hammer till something useful falls out.” Of course all the non-determinism that plagues their conclusions would be solved by a well constructed semantic layer. I really think that overusing the reasoning (aka looping) methodology of inference time compute is a very inelegant way of solving this issue. The disconnect of LLM champions and the database folks is still a gulf that is both frustrating and a great opportunity if you know where to look.

Small but Cool

https://x.com/topazlabs

This is really just a compute ramped version of diffusion based image super-resolution, but I like the concept. I’m sure this will (probably already does) have lots of competitors. Still, the opportunity to make/remake old films, documentaries and home videos will be really mind blowing. BUT, I’m in before someone uses this on the JFK assassination video and we have a whole new theory on how it was indeed a Sesame Street character from the grassy knoll.

Workflows with LLMs

https://computer.tldraw.com/

This is really fun and free. I’ve been thinking about using something like this in my kids’ education. It’s like a flow diagram for pseudocode, except that it uses basic LLM prompts to execute steps in the chain. While I don’t feel like (have the “vibe”) that something like this is where things are headed for LLM based workflows in the long run it is a great concept for people to understand what the current process looks like and to build some thought processes around the use of LLMs.
