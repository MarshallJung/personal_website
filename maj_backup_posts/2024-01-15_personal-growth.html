Personal Growth

Personal growth and self improvement is liberating, fulfilling and exciting. But it’s also a trap that convinces you that you’re somehow unfinished and that life isn’t something to enjoy living quite yet. Always just out of touch and right around the corner. Just one more thing to accomplish. People who are driven to accomplish have mastered a sacrifice-reward dynamic that is useful in the micro but damaging in the macro. Gurwinder Bhogal says, “Deferred Happiness Syndrome is the common feeling that your life has not begun, that your present reality is a mere prelude to some idyllic future. This idyll is a mirage that’ll fade as you approach, revealing that the prelude you rushed through was in fact the one to your death.” It would be wise to cultivate the habit of taking time each day to be grateful for what each of us does have, the things we’ve accomplished, our family and friends, our faith. Those that can stop right where they are, happy in the moment while never letting their eyes fall from the horizon will be the truly happy.

How do you define human consciousness? If I was to ask you right now to explain your favorite subject to me would you have every detail memorized? While it’s impossible for me to know how you think, I can tell you that I do not have every detail of race car suspension geometry memorized. Rather I start at a single point (perhaps a question about static vs. dynamic roll center interactions) and scale out from there. Is my understanding compressed and extensible rather than completely memorized? So the question then is, “Is there an optimum sparse rate reduction methodology for every human, or is it unique”
