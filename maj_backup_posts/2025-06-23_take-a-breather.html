Take a Breather

It’s been several months since major AI labs released new foundational models, and recent updates have mainly been iterations of existing ones. This pause seems to result from a mix of economics, strategy, and public sentiment. Aside from Altman’s “Gentle Singularity” talk, throwing shade at Elon, and Meta apparently offering $100M comp packages to lure top AI talent, my theory is that the AI industry is in a strategic breather, consolidating gains after a frenzied investment period. The cost of training large models is high, and current returns aren’t justifying the expense, particularly with venture capital hesitant to fund speculative ventures without clear ROI. While the market isn’t saturated, there’s a shift towards applications, with companies investing in fine-tuning existing models for real-world uses, such as healthcare, exemplified by a recent $40 million investment in Sword Health. (https://www.marketscreener.com/news/latest/Sword-Health-valued-at-4-billion-in-General-Catalyst-led-funding-round-50261841/) There is also ongoing concern from large-scale failures like Builder.ai’s incident, keeping investors and regulators cautious. The technology hasn’t plateaued, but diminishing returns are becoming evident and the capabilities of the tools haven’t been nearly defined or sorted as of yet. It will be interesting to see how this evolves once we take a breather and assess future investment opportunities.

Exercise is Good, Even for your Brain

https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08872

The study found that lower brain connectivity during LLM use indicates reduced internal attention and memory rehearsal. Essays generated with ChatGPT showed high homogeneity, tightly clustered in the embedding space with repeated named entities. Additionally, habitual ChatGPT users exhibited low connectivity and memory when asked to write without assistance, indicating lingering cognitive debt.

This is the second study I’ve mentioned in my newsletters regarding the impact of large language models on intellectual work. Like the previous study, it shows that relying on these AI tools can reduce human creativity and critical thinking. While there may be more papers with similar findings, and two studies aren’t enough for replication, the findings are concerning. The problem comes from the unfortunate reality of humans tendency towards laziness. When used as a tutor or a debate partner, AI can be exceptionally helpful in helping understand new ideas an topics, but it’s too easy to use them as a crutch to simply do the work. From personal experience, it’s crucial to develop critical thought processes through writing and engaging discussions with others. This gets back to my concerns on the “greying” of human knowledge that I wrote about a six months back (https://x.com/marshalljung/status/1886849786642636879). Don’t let AI crush the beautiful range of human creativity. Don’t become too reliant!

A Rare Glimpse

In March, the New Scientist reported a Freedom of Information request they filed to access the ChatGPT logs of the UK Secretary of State for Science, Innovation, and Technology. https://www.newscientist.com/article/2472068-revealed-how-the-uk-tech-secretary-uses-chatgpt-for-policy-advice/

We don’t see the raw data from the request, but it provides a rare glimpse into how the average person uses AI tools (yea, politicians are probably largely average in IQ, there I said it). Aside from your own history, you likely wouldn’t know how others are utilizing them unless you directly observe them, which is probably quite uncommon. Leaving aside the ethics of using AI tools to help craft policy, I noticed two main issues with the Minister’s questions presented in the article. First, he assumes that AI tools have an omniscience that they do not possess. I would expect the minister’s prompts to generate an average of what has been published on a subject based on the training data up to the cutoff date, but that is clearly not what the question is after. Second, there’s a misunderstanding of how these models are trained; if a correct answer isn’t in the pre-cutoff data, the response will likely be unsatisfactory. While some deep research tools can help, they still risk falling into the “garbage in, garbage out” scenario if the related information is poorly represented in an internet search.

Benchmarks!

https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18878

Salesforce’s effort to establish a new benchmark for realistic business tasks is interesting and useful for enterprises. While currently focused on coding, these benchmarks are valuable for assessing return on investment and overall impact in the business space. It will be fascinating to see how these evolve over time. However, it’s essential to ensure that test data remains limited to avoid compromising the integrity of the benchmark standings.
