### BlackHat

[https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=87DyyMV0kCY&t=1088s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=87DyyMV0kCY&t=1088s)

Last week, OpenAI gave a Black Hat keynote detailing the HuggingFace incident. I covered this in a newsletter a few weeks ago, but the talk adds new insights. It’s worth watching even if you’re not into tech or security in particular. At around 18 minutes, they show how the agents communicate while escaping their sandbox and executing the HuggingFace hack.

As I noted in that recent newsletter, the autonomy of frontier models (and the agentic systems they can coordinate) is becoming unsettling. Given any opening, even inadvertent, to achieve a goal, they will exploit it. The intelligence of individual AI agents isn’t the constraint as individual instances spontaneously cooperate and can spawn huge numbers of agents to explore all possible avenues. It’s very hard to anticipate what smart, cooperating AIs can do when pursuing an ultimate goal.

This type of AI is crucial for positive outcomes in cybersecurity, national defense, and advancing scientific fields. Yet the same tools can cause significant harm, economically and militarily. With great power comes great responsibility.

### Agents are still rare

Many harness products from OpenAI, Anthropic, and others mask agent use and drive adoption, yet getting the most from agents remains a rare skill. People fixated on chatbot interaction methodology and miss that working with an agent is more like managing a process than asking questions. Prompting an agent is closer to writing a specification. You must scope the task extensively and define what “done” looks like.

The upside comes from changing the workflow itself instead of treating agents as another Q&A system. That means giving agents the right data, crossing organizational boundaries, and evolving human-in-the-loop review. Enterprises must rethink current processes and reset political and informational silos to realize agentic AI’s benefits.

As a result, most future enterprise token usage will likely come from agents executing tasks within workflows, not from employees asking chatbots questions.

### I Am Levi

![Figure](/images/blog/2026-08-10-black-hat/image-04.png)

[https://github.com/ConMoo/ChatCFD](https://github.com/ConMoo/ChatCFD)

AI has become a powerful tool in my personal engineering work, especially for optimizing my race car. I’ve written before about text-to-CAD and plan to revisit it soon as it’s progressed a lot recently. I’m also exploring AI “loops” to optimize designs with existing engineering software tools. In that vein, the open-source GitHub project ChatCFD is both cleverly named and a practical way to interact with the MCP protocols from OpenFOAM, the open-source CFD package you can run on your desktop.

![Figure](/images/blog/2026-08-10-black-hat/image-05.png)

I used ChatCFD with input from my 3D scanner and printer to design experimental wing-profile canards for the front of the race car to gain a bit more front downforce. The exact technical details aren’t important, but the results of the process were meaningful.

And the results of this process aren’t about the race car, the software, or the techniques. The lesson is humility. This work comes easily to me, likely because it’s my passion and I’m naturally curious. Most of what I know wasn’t taught to me in school. I learned by following my fascination and practicing until I became skilled. But my father reminded me that calling these processes easy and straightforward isn’t true for most people. And this is a blind spot for me. God gives us gifts, but He isn’t the only one who knows I have this kind of intelligence. Satan knows too. Too easily my gifts turn into pride, which breeds impatience and arrogance. Roadblocks and speed bumps in my spiritual life.

Humility, for me, means recognizing that in my interactions with others I’m called to be a servant first and a contributor second. The world needs my skills and contributions. I’ve been given the gifts of intelligence and curiosity. But like Levi, the successful tax collector, I sometimes need to set aside my job, worldly passions, and success to follow Jesus and his message of humility and servant leadership. Ultimately, the rewards of this life are very temporary.
