Apple and OpenAI

I wrote about this a few months ago when the rumors of a partnership first emerged. It was always a bit of a mystery why Apple wasn’t doing something big and public with AI given their extensive and loyal user base. It seems that Apple is betting more on AIs based around lightweight reasoning and very good local retrieval/ low hallucination rates, while OpenAI is betting on autonomous agents. I do wonder how this angle will shape the perception of AI in the mind of the public given that it will likely appear to be only a slight improvement over existing assistant experiences like Siri.

There are some other considerations. Privacy is a major one. Musk tweeted that Apple devices will be banned from his facilities, and Apple is well known for privacy. Partnering with OpenAI for data transfer is a big step outside that secure walled garden. Similar to the MSFT issue with it’s OS embedded Copilot AI that is already showing some security holes, this will make major corporations very nervous and certainly give the security and compliance people a lot of work hashing out appropriate policies.

Total Recall

[https://www.wired.com/story/total-recall-windows-recall-ai/](https://www.wired.com/story/total-recall-windows-recall-ai/)

As previewed in the section above, MSFT’s on device, OS native AI has already been shown to store data locally in an unencrypted database. Given the existence of privilege escalation attacks it’s not too hard to imagine that someone can gain access to this local DB and use it for data mining around PII, passwords and other sensitive information. MSFT says that it can be customised or disabled, but again perception is reality. Will the AI be useful enough to overcome the perception of a foreign agent looking over your shoulder constantly? Less a technological problem and more of a human psychological one.

More uncertainty in the benchmark space?

As the paper states, “This study highlights a striking discrepancy between LLMs’ high scores on standardized reasoning benchmarks (e.g., MMLU, ARC, Hellaswag) and their poor performance on the AIW problem, suggesting that current benchmarks do not adequately reflect models’ true reasoning capabilities and weaknesses.”

[https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.02061](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.02061)

This is a rather long paper really exploring a single prompt, but does show reasoning abilities and common sense should not be confused with an ability to store and approximately retrieve facts.

Hallucination is inevitable

[https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.11817](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.11817)

This work challenges the assumption that hallucinations can be completely eliminated with sufficient data or advanced techniques, highlighting a fundamental limitation in LLMs that must be acknowledged and managed rather than eradicated. Again this points to an application awareness rather than a problem to be solved (which the paper presents as a mathematical proof) Hallucinations are \*not\* necessarily limitations of LLMs. The same hallucination that generates interesting art also becomes the problem in a retrieval or summarization task. Thus, it’s the use case that makes hallucinations problematic, not the property itself.

A quick tip for those fellow Fusion360 folks

Use canvases in your sketches to import a photograph of things you want to model. You can then calibrate the image to a known measurement and build from there. Always keep in mind parallax! I’m adding a rear wing to the race car and after going through the CAD (Cardboard Aided Design) to build the chassis mount stanchions, I took a photo and imported it as a canvas and sketched the rest from there. Then off to SendCutSend and it’s as easy as pie.
