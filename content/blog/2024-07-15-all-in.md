### A tweak to my concepts

[https://bmcbioinformatics.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/1471-2105-12-334](https://bmcbioinformatics.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/1471-2105-12-334)

So one of the difficulties that I’ve found in understanding how to store and generate 3D objects using transformer based models is how to store the training data. Sure, 3D vector space is how we all usually think about objects in the real world, but size of this data and the indeterminate scale and orientation is something that needs to be done in a data pipeline ahead of time. This challenge combined with the observation that one “unit” of an object has effects, continuity or relationships with those around it lead me to this spatial indexing paper that explores 3D interactions in the micro-world. Think about being able to encode the 3D relationships in a transformer layer in a way that adheres to physical laws, but keeps the benefits of language model generation.

### I’ve already said it

[arxiv.org/abs/2406.05184](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.05184)

Will training on AI-generated synthetic data lead to the next frontier of vision models? The key idea being, synthetic data isn’t truly new data. All synthetic data derives from the original (real) data used to train a generative model. To quantify the actual “value-add” of synthetic data, it must be compared against using this original data directly. In this paper they make this comparison explicit. On five image recognition benchmarks, they compare CLIP models fine tuned on either: Synthetic images generated Stable Diffusion, which was trained on the LAION-2B dataset or original real images directly retrieved from LAION-2B. Overall, they show that while training on synthetic data can improve models (which is consistent with earlier findings from other research), training on original real data helps much more across all settings/scales considered. With current best methods, synthetic images do not yield added value.
