This past weekend, I was reminded of the importance of gratitude in our lives, especially towards those who help us during difficult times or inspire us when our own spirit gives up. Reflecting on my life, I’ve been fortunate to have many people who uplifted me when I was down, offering their time and support to help me move forward when I felt unable to do it on my own. From my brother helping me with my racecar when I needed the project most, to my twin for sharpening my spirit when I felt lost in worldly confusion. I am grateful for my wife being strong and forgiving in spirit and my family for patience and acceptance when it would have been much easier to simply walk away.  
For everyone reading this letter, take the time to be grateful for the friends and family in your life. The ones that have stuck around and stuck through. The ones that have given tough love and grace. It is those individuals that show the Divine in their actions. Hold them close and tell them that you love them. These are the greatest blessings, the greatest wealth, that anyone on this earth could hope to secure.

### The week of Ghibli Madness

![Figure](/images/blog/2025-03-31-gratitude-for-others/image-01.jpg)

If you follow machine learning or AI social media last week, you probably noticed OpenAI’s recent launch of its multimodal image generation capabilities. You were probably overwhelmed. The Studio Ghibli memes were funny for a while, but the real news is how these images were generated. Gemini employs a similar concept but primarily utilizes an auto-regressive generation methodology, unlike the classic image generation method, which is based on diffusion. Using autoregressive algorithms, the resulting images are typically more accurate to the user’s prompt, particularly in text and image positioning, compared to previous methods. I have two thoughts on this. First, there’s an intriguing concept regarding copyrighted materials, as it appears to simplify the process of transferring artistic styles. This highlights the growing importance of copyright and intellectual property, particularly since visual arts often engage more emotionally with this issue than writing. Currently, note that all images generated by OpenAI’s new methodology include a watermark that contains all associated metadata related to the generation and iteration of each image. [https://c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/1.3/specs/\_images/Overview\_Diagram.svg](https://c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/1.3/specs/_images/Overview_Diagram.svg)

![Figure](/images/blog/2025-03-31-gratitude-for-others/image-02.jpg)

Second, at what point do we recognize that AI-generated content is good enough to be considered an artifact in its own right? A video by Fireship discussed the concept of “Slop’s Razor,” suggesting that we may not need to disclose AI generation if the content is either high-quality enough to pass for human creation or low-quality enough that it’s evident it wasn’t made by a human. This could be a pivotal moment for public awareness, as multimodal systems are transforming visual creation. They offer new capabilities while also raising important questions about creative ownership and authenticity. The distinction between humans and AIs will continue to blur, prompting us to rethink what constitutes originality. Many creative professionals may find themselves challenged to reassess their perspectives.

### ARC-AGI 2

[https://arcprize.org/blog/announcing-arc-agi-2-and-arc-prize-2025](https://arcprize.org/blog/announcing-arc-agi-2-and-arc-prize-2025)

The second artificial general intelligence, developed by Francois Chollet, was introduced last week. Building on ARC-AGI 1, which was largely saturated by existing reasoning models, this new set of tests aims to be as straightforward for humans as possible, while becoming progressively more challenging for AI algorithms. The goal is to demonstrate true fluid intelligence rather than relying on brute-force solutions. I have a small bone to pick with Chollet’s announcement though. He says, “…in the age of test-time search, it’s pretty much always possible to reach any level of capability by simply expending more compute.

So it’s not just about, “can you do it?”. The key is how efficiently you can do it. Intelligence is not just the ability to demonstrate high skill. It’s the efficiency with which you acquire and deploy these skills. Both data efficiency and compute efficiency.”

I believe he understands my objection, but I want to clarify that test time compute is beneficial only when verification is easier than generation. For example, with Sudoku, solving is straightforward. However, if you’re trying to find a specific fact, like the start of the Revolutionary War, and you don’t know it, no amount of reasoning will yield the correct answer. This highlights the importance of having a strong base model. A 2 billion parameter model cannot match the intelligence of a 20 billion parameter model, even if both are trained on the same data, simply by thinking for a longer period.

### The Age of Agents

Following my visit to NVIDIA’s GTC 2025 conference, I discussed the concept of agents with a consulting company last week. This is a one of those consulting organizations that pays a few hundred dollars for an hour of your time to discuss topics in your area of expertise. For readers of my newsletter who are part of larger organizations, I’d like to summarize some key points from my discussions.. Here’s a brief list, and I’m sure we can add more.

Technical Integration: Ensuring agents can interact across diverse systems (interoperability) and access data from legacy environments.

Security & Compliance: Managing data access permissions and navigating evolving regulatory standards for AI use.

Operational Shift: Determining the appropriate level of agent autonomy versus human oversight and managing the organizational changes required for adoption.

Maturity & Economics: Improving AI model reliability for critical tasks and establishing stable business models for agent deployment.

I think a nuanced, and well thought through approach to deploying AI can enhance a company’s productivity and profitability while also preserving employee enthusiasm and participation during the potentially daunting transition to AI in today’s world.
