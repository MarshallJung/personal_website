*Choose your weapon!*

Over the last week I’ve spent more time with customers than in the past six months combined. There is a general and marked increase in the number of enterprise customers interested in Tabnine as a tool to increase productivity and add value to their developers’ tech stack. While Tabnine has a hard working and far reaching PR team, the truth of the piqued interest lies in a technology that exploded onto the scene in the last few weeks…ChatGPT.

Even for folks living under the proverbial mineral agglomeration (aka “rock”) ChatGPT made it into the mainstream news within two weeks, which even for a game changing technology is an astounding hype cycle. I think it’s important to break down what ChatGPT is and how technologies like Tabnine and ChatGPT work from a very basic perspective. Hopefully this will give folks the ability to sort fact from fiction in the noisy tech space and begin to formulate questions on what tool might be right for your enterprise.

First, let me say that Tabnine and ChatGPT are not competitors. Referencing the opening photograph in this blog; if Tabnine is a wrench, ChatGPT is a hammer (or vice versa) and in application they have totally different jobs. Both can be classified as Generative AI tools, but one is a general knowledge chatbot and Tabnine is a focused code productivity tool. For enterprise customers looking to add 15–25% productivity to a developer team, Tabnine can get you there via SaaS or on-prem, but it definitely isn’t going to give you recipes for dumplings.

Both ChatGPT and Tabnine are built on a technology called transformer networks that got their start with a whitepaper released in 2017 ([https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762)) called “Attention Is All You Need”. Over time, there have been tweaks to this NN method but the astounding increase in the perceived “intelligence” of tools using transformers is due to the increase in computing power available to train these algorithms and the corresponding geometric growth in the size of the datasets fed into the front end of the process. For some of the generic chatbots that dataset has become quite literally everything. Good, useful, bad and meaningless, all data is shoved into the front end training procedure and typically something mostly useful is a result. But push too hard on these chatbots and you’ll find they are “an inch deep and a mile wide” and certainly cannot replace a human expert in any field of knowledge. But that isn’t to say they can never be useful. Curated, QC’d and discriminated datasets can really show the power of transformer based Generative AI, but that effort still requires a human touch.

And this is where the key differentiation starts to diverge between different tools. It’s also why, when I’m with customers, I focus so heavily on data quality and provenance. Both a wrench and a hammer are made of steel, but each is alloyed differently and forged into a different shape in order to accomplish a specific task very effectively. Similarly, transformer algorithms are the raw steel. Feed them different datasets and you can create different tools that can accomplish specific tasks effectively. For our Pro users, Tabnine has specifically chosen high-quality and fully permissive open-source libraries to train our advanced transformers. For our Enterprise customers we can do the same (on-prem or in VPC) with internal codebases to make an even more effective and fit-for-purpose tool for the developer teams.

In summary, transformer based “Generative AI” tools are all pretty much the same under the algorithmic hood, but perform very differently when trained with different datasets. When evaluating these tools for an enterprise ignore the hype and ask yourself where the data used to train this tool was acquired. Most of the time, you’ll be able to sort fact from fiction and evaluate potential value to your company based on the answer to this simple question.
